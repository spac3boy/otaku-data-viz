How To Read The Bubble Map

Every circle represents a game or a paired release. Horizontal position shows release year. The vertical axis changes with the selected view, letting the same catalog become a title list, a franchise comparison, or a history of Nintendo hardware.

Bubble size represents reported sales where a reliable figure is available. LCD color identifies a broad genre family, while a dashed bubble outline marks a title without a verified sales figure. Labels stay attached to their bubbles so dense parts of the catalog remain identifiable while scrolling and zooming.

What The Nintendo Catalog Data Reveals

The curated map contains 406 releases across 100 franchise labels and 13 hardware eras from 1979 through 2026. These findings describe this visualization’s editorial catalog, not Nintendo’s complete worldwide release history.

Switch Has The Largest Hardware-Era Group

The Switch era contains 75 curated releases, more than Nintendo 3DS at 36 and Nintendo DS at 35. That concentration reflects both the length and breadth of the Switch period and the catalog’s emphasis on Nintendo-developed, published, and platform-defining games.

Pokémon Leads The Represented Franchises

Pokémon appears on 35 catalog records, followed by Mario with 32, Zelda with 28, and Kirby with 25. Paired versions may share one record, while remakes and materially distinct editions can appear separately.

Platform Games Form The Largest Genre

Platform games account for 73 releases in the catalog. RPG follows with 58, while Adventure contributes 40. These broad editorial tags make very different eras comparable without treating genre boundaries as absolute.

The 2000s Are The Densest Catalog Decade

The map includes 122 releases from the 2000s, compared with 99 from the 2010s and 73 from the 1990s. This measures representation inside the curated dataset, not Nintendo’s total output during each decade.

How To Use The Visualization

  • Change the vertical axis: use the three pixel-icon tabs for Games, Franchises, and Consoles.
  • Open filters: tap the filter icon or press SELECT, then narrow the catalog by search, era, genre, franchise, and release role.
  • Inspect a game: click or tap any bubble to open its LCD-style detail panel and cover image.
  • Move through the catalog: scroll directly or use the D-pad; after a detail panel is open, D-pad navigation updates the selected game.
  • Zoom the map: use the A and B buttons to zoom in and out while keeping direct touch and mouse controls available.
  • Refresh the chart: press START to replay the loading sequence and redraw the current view.
  • Preview cover art: select a game thumbnail to open a larger image, then close the modal to return to the chart.

Why This Matters For Fans

Nintendo history is usually split across console lists, franchise wikis, sales reports, and individual game pages. This project puts those perspectives in one place without flattening the catalog into a leaderboard.

That makes it useful for spotting forgotten series, comparing generations, following the growth of a franchise, or understanding how software helped define each Nintendo platform.

Methodology And Data Note

The embedded catalog contains 406 Nintendo-developed, Nintendo-published, and selected hardware-defining releases from 1979 through 2026. Paired versions such as Pokémon releases may share one bubble, while remakes and materially distinct editions appear separately. The counts shown on this landing page are calculated directly from that embedded catalog.

This is a curated history of Nintendo’s own catalog and platform identity, not a database of every third-party title released for Nintendo hardware. The inclusion rules prioritize Nintendo’s development and publishing role, recurring properties, historical importance, and representation across hardware eras.

Reported sales are used only when a defensible figure is available, with Nintendo investor materials preferred for modern first-party titles. Cover art is loaded on demand from matching Wikipedia article data and may be unavailable when a stable image cannot be matched. Dates, roles, franchise assignments, and sales figures remain open to correction as sources improve.

Last updated: August 5, 2026. Bubble size indicates reported sales, not critical quality or cultural importance.

FAQ

What is the Nintendo Game Universe Map?

It is a fan-made interactive bubble chart that organizes 406 Nintendo-developed, published, and hardware-defining releases by year, franchise, console era, genre, and reported sales.

Which Nintendo games are included?

The catalog emphasizes Nintendo-developed games, Nintendo-published games, and selected releases that defined Nintendo hardware. It is a curated history rather than a list of every third-party game ever released on Nintendo systems.

How do the bubbles work?

Horizontal position represents release year. Vertical position changes with the Games, Franchises, and Consoles view. Bubble size represents verified reported sales when available, and LCD shades identify genre families.

Why does the visualization look like a Game Boy?

The Game Boy-inspired interface makes Nintendo hardware history part of the interaction. Its D-pad, A and B buttons, START, and SELECT controls supplement direct mouse, keyboard, and touch input.

Is this an official Nintendo project?

No. It is an unofficial fan-made visualization for exploration, commentary, analysis, and fan discussion. Otaku Data Viz is not affiliated with or endorsed by Nintendo.
